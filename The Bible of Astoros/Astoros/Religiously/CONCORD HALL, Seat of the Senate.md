**Type:** The remnant of the First; sole neutral ground shared by all twelve gods  
**Symbol:** A closed door with no handle on either side  
**Colors:** No fixed colors — described as lit by no visible source  
**Access:** Unknown to mortals; the twelve arrive without describing how  
Concord Hall is not a temple built to a god — it is what was left of one. When the First divided its attention into the twelve, whatever remained of it didn't die; it became this place, a room said to be vast beyond its outer walls, arranged around twelve equal seats with no seat set above the rest. No portfolio holds more weight here than any other — light doesn't outshine dark within it, chaos can't unsettle it, death carries no more authority than any other seat's business. Several independent accounts, gathered centuries and cultures apart, describe an empty thirteenth space at the center of the circle that none of the twelve have ever explained.

The Hall's _existence_ isn't disputed by anyone, mortal or divine. Its _location_ is. Once reachable by an actual pilgrimage road, that road no longer leads anywhere, and no one who has claimed to find another way has returned to prove it. Doorkeepers and Threadbound believe the location was deliberately let go, once a fixed and findable seat of divine power became a liability. Bound Readers suspect it simply drifted "slightly outside the world," as the oldest texts already described it, until the question of where stopped making sense to ask. Curtain-Keepers insist it was never lost at all, only buried under an age of changed geography, and their Seekers still test that belief on foot in remote, unmapped ranges — returning, each time, with nothing but a story about a storm that turned them back.
