###### **A century ago, East and West Masterg were a single dukedom, ruled from a fortress that still stands — half in ruin — along the mountain spine that now divides them. The split didn't come from war with outsiders; it came from two brothers who each believed the ducal seat belonged to them, and a court too evenly divided to settle the matter without bloodshed. Rather than let the dukedom tear itself apart entirely, the line was drawn along the mountains themselves, and two states were born out of a family that could no longer share a table.**


**East Masterg** kept the old capital, the deeper mines, and — its people insist — the rightful claim to the original ducal seal, though West Masterg disputes this at every opportunity. East Masterg leans harder into old tradition: formal titles, ancestral banners, and a court that still observes ceremonies most of the continent considers outdated. Its wealth comes from what the mountains produce rather than what its ports could move, and its people take a certain pride in being self-sufficient in a way Illipand, with all its gold, never quite manages to be.

**West Masterg**, cut off from the old capital, built something newer and hungrier — a court that cares less about lineage than results, and a reputation for producing shrewd, occasionally ruthless administrators who've found work advising half the smaller courts on Malagaerûn. West Masterg never stopped considering itself the true heir of the old dukedom, just one that had to earn its legitimacy the hard way rather than inherit it.

The two states share a border, a bloodline, and an old grudge that shows no sign of fading — skirmishes over the mountain passes flare up every few years, more ritual than real war at this point, settled with as much bluster as blood. Neither state has any interest in Chatillinquis's growing shadow to the east; each is far more concerned with proving, generation after generation, that it is the true Masterg and the other merely a pretender wearing the family name.
