Two generations ago, Chatillinquis was a modest trading principality, respected for its craftsmen and its position along the northern trade roads but unremarkable in every other way. What changed was a single ambitious ruler who understood that conquest didn't require an army if you were patient enough to let debt and marriage do the work first. Chatillinquis's expansion has followed the same pattern for decades now: a struggling neighbor takes a Chatillinquis loan, a Chatillinquis bride or groom marries into the local nobility, and within a generation the "independent" state finds its decisions quietly being made from Chatillinquis's court instead of its own.

Where debt and marriage haven't worked, Chatillinquis has not hesitated to use the sword — and its military, built on the wealth its economic conquests generated, has grown formidable enough that few of its neighbors can any longer afford to call its bluff. The empire's official doctrine is "unification under the crown," framed as a restoration of some older, more prosperous age when the northern peoples were supposedly one people. Historians in Yonia have quietly noted that this "older age" appears nowhere in any record predating Chatillinquis's own propaganda office — a detail Chatillinquis has never bothered to dispute, because by the time anyone checks, it no longer matters.

Chatillinquis's greatest asset may be its patience. Unlike a conqueror who marches and burns, Chatillinquis's empire feels, from the inside, almost gentle — occupied nobles keep their titles, occupied peoples keep their customs, and life often changes little in the first years after annexation. The change comes later, slowly: taxes redirected, local militias absorbed into the imperial army, court appointments increasingly requiring a Chatillinquis education. By the time a conquered people fully understands what's happened to them, resistance is no longer a realistic option.

Kancandrith is the empire's current obsession, and for good reason — it's the last significant buffer between Chatillinquis and the fractured, harder-to-swallow kingdoms further south. Chatillinquis has already tried debt and marriage on several of Kancandrith's minor lords, with mixed success; the hill country's fierce local superstition and its patchwork of tiny, stubborn holdings make the old playbook harder to run than usual. Whether Chatillinquis eventually takes Kancandrith through patience or finally loses patience and takes it by force is, at this point, a question of when rather than if.
