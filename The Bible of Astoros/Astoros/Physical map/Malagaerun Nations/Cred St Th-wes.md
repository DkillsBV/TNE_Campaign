Cred St Th-wes has turned a lack of loyalty into a genuine art form. This port city-state holds itself apart from Malagaerûn's larger conflicts by design — its harbors are open to every flag on the continent, its merchants will trade with anyone who can pay, and its famously mercenary companies have, at various points, fought for and against nearly every major power on Malagaerûn, sometimes within the same decade. Neutrality here isn't a moral position; it's a business strategy, refined over generations into something close to a civic religion.

The city's government reflects that philosophy — a council of merchant-captains and guild-heads who govern with one overriding priority: keeping the harbor open and profitable, regardless of who's currently winning whatever war happens to be raging elsewhere on the continent. Cred St Th-wes has hosted diplomatic talks between bitter enemies, sold weapons to both sides of at least one Masterg border skirmish, and never once let any of it interfere with its own peace. Its council views this not as hypocrisy but as professionalism.

This reputation makes Cred St Th-wes simultaneously useful and untrustworthy to everyone who deals with it. Chatillinquis uses its harbors to move supplies the empire would rather not transport through more scrutinized routes; Illipand's trade houses launder awkward transactions through Cred St Th-wes brokers; even Kancandrith's suspicious minor lords have been known to hire the city's mercenary companies when their own militias aren't enough. Everyone uses Cred St Th-wes. No one fully trusts it, and the city has never seen much reason to change that.

Socially, Cred St Th-wes is one of the more cosmopolitan places on Malagaerûn — its docks see travelers, exiles, and fortune-seekers from every nation on the continent, and the city asks few questions about where anyone came from as long as their coin is good. That openness has made it a natural place for people who don't fit comfortably anywhere else to wash up and stay, provided they can find a way to be useful to the harbor's endless churn of trade.
