## Backstory

Antwin didn't start as one city — it started as two villages that happened to be looking at each other across a river.

The west bank came first. When Grael's earliest prospectors traced the ice-touched ore vein down out of the highlands, it ran straight into the low bluffs above the ford, and the camp that grew up there to work the seam became **Ashthorpe** — little more than a scatter of shaft-heads and smithies at first, but old enough that by the time the camp had a name, it also had a government hall to argue in, the same squat black-stoned building that still stands on the Ashthorpe–Brighthorpe line today, older than the Republic itself.

The east bank came second, and for a very different reason. Ore is heavy, rivers are useful, and someone on the far shore realized long before anyone on the west bank did that the fastest way to move raw ore downstream was to drag it across on a rope-ferry and load it onto barges from a proper landing. That landing grew into **Newpole**, named for the mooring post driven into the riverbed to hold the ferry line — a post that, centuries later, still marks the district's central square, now ringed by the counting-houses and warehouses of the merchants who inherited that first ferryman's instincts.

For generations the two banks were rivals as much as partners — Ashthorpe dug it, Newpole shipped it, and both sides argued constantly about who was cheating whom on the exchange rate. It wasn't affection that finally joined them, but economics: ore theft on the ferry crossing got expensive enough that both banks agreed to fund a stone bridge and a shared wall, and Antwin as a single city was born the day that wall closed the loop around both settlements.

When Grael's senate later named Antwin its capital, the city that had spent centuries growing inward suddenly had to grow outward instead — fast. The result is visible at a glance: everything inside the old wall has had generations to settle into its final shape, while the districts crowding right up against the outside of that wall — **Wesgras**, **Glentown**, **Losove**, **Olard**, and **Wind Meadow** — are recent enough that half of them are still technically farmland with houses on it. Grael's senators, characteristically, have not yet agreed on who should pay to wall them in too.

## Inside the Wall — West Bank (Old Ashthorpe)

### Ashthorpe

The oldest ground in the city, and it shows — the district is dense with artisan workshops that trace their lineages back to the original mining camp, clustered tightly around the old black-stoned Government Hall that predates the Republic. **The Founder's Hall** is still where west-bank civic business gets done, even though the real senate now sits across the river.

### Litollow

An artisan district in its own right, grown up as Ashthorpe's workshops ran out of room. **Deepvein Row** is a stretch of smithies and tool-makers who supply the mines directly, and the whole district smells faintly of coal smoke on a still day.

### Wind Circle

The plaza that grew up around the original river ford, before the bridge existed — still the busiest crossing point in the city, ringed with shops, taverns, and the odd shrine catching trade from travelers heading in every direction. **The Ferryman's Rest** is a tavern built, according to local legend, from the timbers of the very last ferry that ran before the bridge made it obsolete.

### Lowvill

A crowded, colorful mix of shops, a small temple district, and enough taverns and inns to suggest this was once the low-lying part of town nobody wanted to build anything permanent in — which is more or less true. **The Sunken Shrine** sits below street level in what used to be a flood channel before the riverbanks were reinforced.

### Brighthorpe

Quieter than its neighbors, sitting in the shadow of Ashthorpe's old Government Hall. Mostly residential, with a scattering of service buildings — the kind of district where senate clerks and hall functionaries actually live.

### Olclear

A mixed residential and artisan quarter with no single defining industry, which locals take a strange pride in — "Olclear makes a bit of everything" is a common saying. **The Clearwater Well**, one of the few in the city not fed by the river directly, is said to never run dry.

### Whitedal

Another artisan-heavy district, southwest and slightly removed from the main thoroughfares. Home to several long-established craft guilds who prefer their workshops away from the noise of Wind Circle.

### Newreach

The newest district still technically inside the old wall — built into the last unclaimed corner of the west bank as Ashthorpe's population outgrew its original footprint. Sparse compared to its neighbors, and still has room to grow.

## Inside the Wall — East Bank (Newpole and the Landing Districts)

### Newpole

The literal and figurative center of the east bank, built around the old ferry mooring post that gave it its name. Now the city's commercial heart, ringed with counting-houses, warehouses, and the guild offices of the merchants who first turned a rope-ferry into a fortune. **The Mooring Post** itself still stands in the central square, preserved more out of stubbornness than sentiment.

### Whitshambles

Historically the district where livestock and raw goods were butchered, sorted, and prepared before sale — the name is an old word for a market row, and the district still leans heavily toward shops and warehouses. **Old Shambles Market** remains one of the largest open markets on the east bank.

### Storaven

A warehouse and service district that grew up to support Newpole's trade — less glamorous, more essential. Most of what passes through Antwin's markets spends at least a night in a Storaven warehouse first.

### Oldorough

One of the east bank's original neighborhoods, predating even the bridge, with a cultural and religious character that Newpole's newer money never quite displaced. **The Old Chapel Row** houses several of the city's older shrines, tucked between newer buildings that grew up around them.

### Grenpol

A quieter, more residential district on the city's southeastern edge, popular with merchants who want to live near their warehouses in Newpole and Storaven without living directly among them.

### Darkmoor

The southernmost district on the east bank, named for the boggy ground it was built on before proper drainage. Has a reputation — not entirely undeserved — for being where the city's less official business gets conducted after dark.

## Outside the Wall — The New Districts

Every district below is younger than the Republic's decision to make Antwin its capital, and it shows: unpaved lanes, half-converted farmland, and a noticeable lack of the stone construction found inside the old wall.

### Wesgras

Still mostly farmland, parceled into holdings that are slowly being subdivided into housing as the city's population keeps climbing faster than anyone planned for.

### Glentown

A small, tightly-packed settlement just south of the wall, mostly housing for workers who commute into Darkmoor and Newpole but haven't found (or can't afford) space inside the walls themselves.

### Losove

North along the river, growing quickly thanks to its water access — likely to become the next district petitioning the senate for a wall extension.

### Olard

Northwest of Losove, smaller and quieter, mostly agricultural still.

### Wind Meadow

The newest and least developed of the outer districts, named — somewhat optimistically — for the open meadow it used to be before anyone started building on it.
