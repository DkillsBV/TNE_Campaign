_The contested lowlands beneath Newin_

### An Alliance of Necessity, Not Trust

Wessyri stands alongside Starn and Listeder as one of Newin's three sworn allies — but the word "sworn" does a great deal of work there. None of the three kingdoms could hold Edwarovii's ambitions or Bricky's pressure alone, and the pact between them exists for exactly that reason and no other. The moment the conversation turns to anything beyond that shared threat, the alliance simply stops applying. Against Starn, against Listeder, against every smaller nation outside the pact entirely, Wessyri is fighting its own quiet war for the crown Newin ultimately controls — and it has never pretended otherwise to anyone at court.

That's a harder position than it sounds. Wessyri sits squeezed against both Edwarovii to the west and Bricky to the south, absorbing pressure from two of the most dangerous forces on the continent while also managing rivalries with the very allies it's sworn to. Where Starn and Listeder each face a single hostile front, Wessyri fights on several at once — a geography that would break most kingdoms, and one Wessyri has learned, deliberately, to turn into its whole political argument.

### Survival as Credential

Newin's court does not reward kingdoms for peace — it rewards them for proving they belong at the table, and Wessyri has built its entire claim to the crown around simply _lasting_. Every season it survives pinned between Edwarovii's campaigns and Bricky's expansion, while still holding its own against Starn and Listeder's maneuvering, is treated in Newin's halls as evidence that Wessyri has earned the crown more thoroughly than either ally — not through charm or bloodline, but through sheer demonstrated endurance. Wessyri's diplomats know this, and play to it shamelessly: every border skirmish becomes a talking point, every season held is another argument for why Newin's favor should tilt their way.

### Its People

Wessyri's population reflects a kingdom that has always had to make do with whoever was willing to fight for it. **Humans** form the bulk of its population and its levies, hardened by generations of fighting on more fronts than anyone else in the pact. **Orcs**, many with old ties to Edwarovii's own borderlands, serve prominently in Wessyri's frontier regiments — mercenary in origin, but increasingly loyal to a crown that's never treated them as expendable the way Edwarovii's own hierarchy sometimes does. **Dwarves** hold Wessyri's fortress-engineering, a discipline the kingdom has had to master out of pure necessity given how much of its border needs constant reinforcement. **Halflings and gnomes** fill its logistics and supply lines, the unglamorous work of keeping an army fed on multiple fronts at once. A smaller population of **tieflings and goliaths** rounds out the kingdom, mercenaries and sellswords drawn by steady work in a land that never seems to run out of fighting to do.

### A Crown Earned by Not Losing

Wessyri keeps no illusions about winning its war for the crown through a single decisive victory — there isn't one to win. Its strategy, both militarily and politically, is one of patient attrition: hold every front long enough, survive every season Edwarovii or Bricky throw at it, and let Newin's court draw its own conclusions about which of the three allies actually deserves what the pact is ultimately for. It's a slow, exhausting way to make a claim to a crown. But Wessyri has never had the luxury of a faster one, and has come to see that as the whole point.
