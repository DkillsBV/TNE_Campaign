_The war-scarred heartland between Manorn Nore and Am Aupeth_

Cangansov has never had the luxury of being left alone. Wedged between the raiding coasts of Manorn Nore, the old elven border of Walingand, and now the swelling ambitions of Am Aupeth to the south, it occupies some of the most fought-over ground on the continent — and its history reads accordingly: a long, grinding record of border wars, occupations, and territory lost as often as it's been reclaimed.

### A History of Being Fought Over

Cangansov was never conquered outright, but it has bled for its independence more than any of its neighbors. For centuries it absorbed raids from Manorn Nore's clans, fought grinding border wars with whatever power held the Wesch and Virk territories at the time, and watched more than one of its own frontier provinces slip away for good. Most painfully, a generation ago the western marches of **Cerrehiven** — worn down by decades of exactly this kind of endless raiding from both Cangansov's rivals and its own overstretched defenses — chose to leave the fight entirely, sealing a royal marriage with Walingand rather than continue bleeding for a border no one seemed able to hold. Cangansov did not lose Cerrehiven to conquest. It lost it to exhaustion, and that loss stings more, not less, for having been voluntary.

That history has produced a national character built almost entirely around one lesson: _no one is coming to save you, and no one else will hold your border for you._

### Armed for the Next War Before It Arrives

Where Walingand relies on patience and Manorn Nore on ferocity, Cangansov has built something neither of them has: a disciplined, standing military maintained continuously rather than raised in crisis. Every able adult trains with the local militia as a matter of course, provincial garrisons are kept manned even in years of peace, and the treasury spends more on fortification and steel than any of its neighbors think is reasonable — until, inevitably, it turns out to have been exactly enough and not a coin more.

This buildup has only accelerated in recent years, as word of Am Aupeth's rapid, doctrine-driven annexations to the south has spread. Cangansov's commanders don't call it paranoia; they call it pattern recognition. A nation that has already lost land to Manorn Nore's raids and Walingand's marriages has no intention of being the next territory some Empress's historians describe as "reunified." Border forts along the southern frontier have been reinforced ahead of any actual threat, veteran officers are being recalled to train new levies, and diplomats have been quietly testing whether old rivals like Wesch, Virk, or even Manorn Nore might be persuaded that a common enemy is worth a temporary peace.

### A Nation, Not a People

Unlike its neighbors, Cangansov was never built around one dominant race — its long history of shifting borders and absorbed frontier provinces has left it a genuine patchwork. **Humans** form the bulk of its population and officer corps, but **dwarves** hold much of its fortress-engineering and heavy industry, **halflings and gnomes** are common in its logistics corps and border villages, and **orcs**, many with roots in old Manorn Nore raiding families that settled rather than returned, serve prominently in its frontier regiments. Even a scattering of **tieflings and elves** can be found in its ranks, drawn less by ideology than by Cangansov's one great unifying promise: whatever else it can't offer, it will fight for the ground you stand on.

To outsiders, Cangansov can look like a nation permanently bracing for a blow that hasn't landed yet. To Cangansov, that's simply what survival has always required — and this time, it intends to be ready before the enemy is at the gate rather than after.
