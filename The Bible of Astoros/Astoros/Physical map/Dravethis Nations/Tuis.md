_The dark ridgelands east of Lansoomeshiz_

### The Landing

Two hundred and fifty years ago, the grey highlands east of what is now Lansoomeshiz belonged to scattered clans loosely tied to Untore's outer reaches — the land to the west was nothing yet either, just unclaimed woodland dotted with the earliest hidden hamlets of refugees who hadn't yet thought to call themselves a nation. Into this came the ships: a fleet of exiles from **Malagaerûm**, a distant continent whose name now survives on Dravethis only in old ship-registries and war-songs, fleeing a homeland collapse their descendants still argue about — a war, a famine, a broken dynasty, depending on whose account you trust. What's certain is that they didn't ask to settle. They took the ridgelands by force, driving off or subjugating the clans already living there within a single brutal generation, and built Tuis on ground that was never theirs to begin with.

### A Nation Built on Conquest, Not Memory

Tuis has never pretended otherwise, at least not to itself. Its oldest songs are as much about the landing campaigns as the voyage itself, and its founding families still hold status directly tied to which beach their ancestors' ships took and how much resistance they broke to keep it. The original highland clans were not erased — many were absorbed, intermarried, or put to work under the new order — but Tuis's institutions, language, and ruling houses are unmistakably Malagaerûmi in origin, imposed rather than blended in from the start.

Malagaerûm itself has never sent word since. No ship has come from the old continent in living memory, and Tuis has stopped expecting one — leaving a nation that conquered its home a quarter-millennium ago, and still isn't entirely sure it's finished being an occupier rather than a homeland.

### Its People

Tuis was settled predominantly by **humans and dwarves** — Malagaerûm's shipwrights and highland warrior-clans, respectively, who made up the bulk of the invading fleet. Dwarves took to Tuis's grey ridgelines quickly, mirroring holds they'd left behind, while human settlers spread through the conquered valleys and coastal landings. A proud minority of **goliaths**, descended from a single allied clan that fought alongside the original invasion, hold particular honor in Tuis's military tradition. The original highland peoples — largely **elves and gnomes** — still live throughout Tuis today, generations after the conquest, occupying an uneasy status: fully part of the nation by any practical measure, but rarely found among its oldest noble houses. A scattering of **tieflings and halflings** has settled in more recently, largely indifferent to a hierarchy that isn't theirs either way.

### Legacy of the Fleet

Tuis's military still carries the shape of an invading force that never stopped organizing like one. Warbands are called **crews**, officers hold naval-derived ranks even in landlocked regiments, and the highest honor remains a place among the **Wake-Kept** — descendants who can trace their line to one of the original invasion ships. It's a title that says a great deal about who Tuis considers a founder, and who it still, quietly, considers conquered.
