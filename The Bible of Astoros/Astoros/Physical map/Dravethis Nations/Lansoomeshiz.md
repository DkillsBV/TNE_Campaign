_The green heartland below Untore_

### The Refuge That Became a Nation

Lansoomeshiz began as nothing at all — dense, unclaimed woodland nobody wanted enough to hold, which made it perfect for people who had nowhere else to go. Over generations it quietly filled with refugees: halflings displaced by Cangansov's endless border wars, gnome artisans fleeing debt or persecution, orcs who'd left Manorn Nore rather than live by the Reckoning, and tiefling families unwelcome anywhere doctrine ruled. Scattered hidden hamlets slowly stitched into villages, then provinces, then — almost by accident — a nation, simply because there were finally enough people here to call it one.

It still governs itself that way: informally, communally, and with a deep, reflexive suspicion of centralized power, since nearly everyone's ancestors fled a throne of some kind. Its "capital" is barely more than the largest village, its leadership a loose council of elder settlers from each founding wave rather than any dynasty.

### Its People

**Halflings and gnomes** make up the bulk of the population — the original waves of settlers, and still the backbone of its farming villages and craft-hamlets. **Orcs** form a sizeable minority in the outer farmsteads and woodcutting camps, many descended from those who walked away from Manorn Nore's Reckoning rather than live under it. A small but settled **tiefling** community lives scattered through the deeper villages, largely unmolested — a rarity they don't take for granted. A handful of **humans and dwarves** round out the population, mostly traders or craftsmen who married in and stayed. No one race rules; that's rather the point.

### The Army

Lansoomeshiz keeps no standing army, no forts, and no uniforms — its defense has always been _not being found_. Instead, every village maintains a **Hollow Watch**: a small band of hunters, trackers, and foragers who know their stretch of woodland better than any invading map ever could. Orc woodsmen and halfling scouts make up most Watches, with gnome-rigged trail traps and false paths doing as much work as any weapon. Villages are deliberately unfortified and widely scattered, so no single strike can cripple more than a handful of families.

When a real threat looms, Watches from neighboring villages quietly link up rather than march to meet it — luring, misdirecting, and picking apart anyone who pushes too deep into land they don't understand. Lansoomeshiz has never won a war by winning a battle. It's won every war so far by making sure no enemy ever finds enough of it to fight.
