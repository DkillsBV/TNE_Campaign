_The northern reach beside Newin_

### Arithmetic, Not Loyalty

Starn's alliance with Newin was never a matter of shared conviction — it was a calculation, and everyone in Starn's court knows exactly which one. Standing alone, Starn could hold off neither Edwarovii's campaigns nor Bricky's steady expansion, and swearing itself to Newin's banner bought something no amount of independent maneuvering could: protection its rivals can't easily punish without provoking the one power on the continent none of them can afford to cross. It's not loyalty. It's the cheapest premium Starn could find for the coverage it needed.

That pact, though, only ever covered the kingdoms outside Newin's own circle. Against Listeder and Wessyri — its fellow sworn allies — and against every smaller nation beyond the pact entirely, Starn is still exactly what it was before signing anything: a rival, quietly maneuvering toward the same crown the other two want, using the alliance's protection as cover to compete rather than as a reason to stop.

### The Reasonable One

Where Wessyri trades on endurance and Listeder presumably plays its own game, Starn has built its entire strategy around a single, carefully cultivated reputation: being the most agreeable member of the pact. Starn is invariably the first to volunteer troops for Newin's causes, the quickest to broker peace whenever Listeder or Wessyri's rivalries threaten to overreach into something Newin's court would frown on, and the most reliably cooperative voice in any dispute that reaches the capital. It costs Starn comparatively little — troops volunteered early are troops that come home with credit attached, and peace brokered by Starn is peace remembered as Starn's doing.

The strategy is not born of genuine peaceableness so much as patient political arithmetic: every time Starn steps in to smooth over a conflict Wessyri or Listeder started or escalated, it reinforces an unspoken narrative at Newin's court — that Starn is the reasonable one, and its rivals are the aggressors who keep needing to be managed. It's a slower path to the crown than open competition, but a far safer one, and Starn has shown no sign of abandoning it.

### Its People

Starn is predominantly **human**, its court culture built around exactly the kind of patient, image-conscious diplomacy the kingdom is known for. **Elves**, drawn by Starn's relative stability and its usefulness as a mediating voice, hold a respected place in its diplomatic corps and advisory councils, well-suited to a kingdom that wins more through negotiation than force. **Halflings and gnomes** fill much of its trade and logistics infrastructure, benefiting from a kingdom that has kept itself deliberately easy to do business with. **Dwarves** maintain Starn's more modest fortifications — sufficient, but never so imposing as to look like preparation for anything but defense. A smaller population of **tieflings and orcs** rounds out the kingdom, generally serving in the volunteer regiments Starn is so quick to offer Newin, where a good showing does as much for a soldier's standing as it does for the crown's reputation.

### An Army Meant to Be Lent

Starn's military, modest by the standards of Edwarovii or even Wessyri, is built less for winning its own wars than for being useful in someone else's. Troops are kept well-drilled and ready specifically so they can be offered to Newin's causes at the first opportunity — a standing gesture of goodwill as much as a fighting force. It is not an army designed to conquer a crown by force. It's one designed to make Starn look like the ally worth crowning, one volunteered campaign at a time.
