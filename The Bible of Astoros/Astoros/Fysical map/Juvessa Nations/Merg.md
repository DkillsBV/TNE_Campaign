_The southeastern mountains and coast of Juvessa_

### Independence by Design

Merg sits on wealth its neighbors would dearly love to control outright — mineral-rich mountains running down to a coastline that would make either Bricky or Frasis a genuinely dominant regional power, if either ever managed to claim it alone. That Merg has stayed independent this long is not an accident of geography or a stroke of good fortune. It's a deliberate, carefully maintained balancing act, and Merg's court treats it as the single most important piece of statecraft the kingdom practices.

The logic is simple and Merg makes sure everyone understands it: Bricky wants Merg's mountains, Frasis wants Merg's coast, and neither can afford to let the other have either without fundamentally shifting the balance of power along the southern reach of the continent. Merg has spent generations making absolutely certain that any move against it looks, from the outside, like a move that hands its wealth to whichever rival didn't make the first move — a guarantee that keeps both neighbors watching each other as closely as they watch Merg itself.

### The Art of Being Unswallowable

Merg's diplomacy is built almost entirely around reinforcing that standoff. It trades generously and visibly with both Bricky and Frasis, careful never to let either relationship look exclusive enough to justify the other invading in response. It has quietly cultivated just enough of its own defensive strength — not to win a war against either neighbor outright, but to make sure any invasion would be costly enough, and slow enough, to give the other rival time to intervene rather than simply celebrate. Merg doesn't need to be stronger than Bricky or Frasis. It only needs to make sure that whichever one moves first hands the other an opportunity too good to pass up.

It's a precarious way to run a kingdom, and Merg's court knows it — the whole arrangement depends on Bricky and Frasis continuing to distrust each other exactly as much as they each want what Merg has. So far, that distrust has proven remarkably durable, and Merg has made it a point of national pride to keep giving both neighbors every reason to maintain it.

### Its People

Merg is predominantly **dwarven**, its mountain communities built on the same mining tradition that makes the kingdom worth fighting over in the first place, tempered by generations of understanding that the mineral wealth beneath their feet is as much a liability as an asset. **Humans** hold much of the coastal towns and trade offices, managing the careful, visible commerce with both Bricky and Frasis that keeps the balancing act functioning. **Gnomes** contribute heavily to Merg's mining engineering and fortification work, turning modest defenses into ones costly enough to make any invasion a serious undertaking. **Halflings** fill out the coastal fishing communities and smaller trade routes, benefiting from a kingdom that has strong incentive to stay open and prosperous with everyone. A smaller population of **tieflings and orcs** rounds out Merg's population, largely mercenaries and specialists retained specifically to make the kingdom's modest army punch above its actual size.

### A Shield, Not a Sword

Merg's military exists for exactly one purpose: making sure any invasion costs more than it's worth, for exactly long enough that a rival's rival gets involved. It isn't built to march anywhere or settle old grievances — Merg has none it particularly cares to settle. Its fortifications, its mountain passes, and its coastal defenses are all designed around delay and cost rather than victory, because Merg has never needed to actually win a war against Bricky or Frasis. It's only ever needed to make sure neither one starts one in the first place.
