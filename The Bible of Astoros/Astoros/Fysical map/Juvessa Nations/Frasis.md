_The coastal reach beside Kirk and Hignolf_

### Neutrality as a Business Model

Frasis has never had the size to compete with its neighbors, and it stopped trying generations ago. Tucked along the coast between Kirk and Hignolf, it found a different kind of leverage entirely: making itself the nation every other party on Juvessa trusts just enough to negotiate through. It's not trust born of affection or shared history — it's trust born of arithmetic. Frasis is simply too small to profit meaningfully from betraying anyone, and every rival power on the continent has quietly done that math and reached the same conclusion. A broker with nothing to gain from tipping the scales is, paradoxically, the safest room in which to argue.

That's not incidental to Frasis's foreign policy. It _is_ Frasis's foreign policy. Neutrality here isn't a principle proclaimed and occasionally bent when convenient — it's a business model, maintained with the same discipline a merchant house applies to its accounts, because the moment Frasis is caught favoring one side, the entire value of being Frasis disappears.

### The Room Where Rivals Talk

Frasis has built its reputation, and its economy, around being useful in exactly this narrow, specific way. Disputes between Hignolf and its neighbors, tensions along Bricky and Merg's contested coastline, even quieter disagreements within Newin's own alliance — a surprising amount of it eventually passes through Frasis's halls, not because Frasis has any power to enforce an outcome, but because it has no stake in which outcome wins. Its diplomats are trained less in persuasion than in restraint — the specific skill of helping two hostile parties reach an agreement without ever appearing to favor either one.

That reputation has made Frasis wealthier and more secure than a nation its size has any right to be. Every kingdom on the continent, from Edwarovii to Newin itself, has at some point needed a venue neither side controls, and Frasis has made certain it's always the obvious answer. It's a strange kind of power — Frasis holds no army capable of enforcing anything, and no wealth to rival Hignolf's mines — but it holds something almost as valuable: a reputation no rival can easily replicate, because replicating it means giving up the very ambitions that make Frasis's neutrality worth something in the first place.

### Its People

Frasis is predominantly **halfling**, its diplomatic culture built on exactly the kind of patient, unassuming trustworthiness the trade demands — a people who have made an art of being easy to underestimate and hard to catch lying. **Gnomes** hold a significant place in Frasis's records offices and legal scholarship, maintaining the exhaustive, meticulously neutral documentation that makes Frasis's brokered agreements hold up long after the ink dries. **Humans**, drawn by steady work in a stable, prosperous nation, fill much of the merchant and administrative class. **Elves**, valued for patience and long institutional memory, serve prominently in Frasis's senior diplomatic corps, often the ones actually seated at the table when a negotiation finally begins. A small population of **tieflings**, comfortable in a nation that judges usefulness over bloodline, works throughout Frasis's legal and mediation offices.

### An Army Kept Small on Purpose

Frasis maintains only a modest defensive militia, and deliberately so — a Frasis capable of real military ambition would stop being a Frasis anyone trusted. Its actual security comes from the simple fact that invading or coercing a neutral broker every other power quietly relies on would cost the aggressor more in continent-wide goodwill than anything Frasis's mountains or coastline could offer in return. Frasis has never needed to be strong. It has only ever needed to remain useful enough that no one can afford the fallout of making it disappear.
