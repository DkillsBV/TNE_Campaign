Sope is small enough that most maps of Malagaerûn barely give it room for its own label, and its people have made a kind of virtue out of that obscurity. It has no navy, no standing army worth mentioning, and no resource valuable enough to justify the cost of conquering it — and that, more than any wall or treaty, has been Sope's entire defense for as long as anyone can remember. Neighboring powers have occasionally glanced at Sope's position and briefly considered absorbing it, only to conclude every time that there's simply nothing there worth the trouble.

What Sope lacks in strategic value, it makes up for in fierce, almost stubborn independence. Its people govern themselves through small local councils rather than any centralized crown, and outside interference — even well-meaning interference — is met with immediate, unified resistance regardless of which of Sope's usually-quarreling villages is affected. It's one of the few things that reliably unites Sope's scattered communities: a shared, near-instinctive refusal to be told what to do by anyone wearing a foreign crest.

Sope's economy is modest and mostly self-sufficient — enough farming, fishing, and small trade to keep its people fed and its councils funded, with little surplus and little ambition to create one. This lack of wealth is, again, part of the design rather than a failure; a poor nation is a nation no one bothers to tax, raid, or annex, and Sope's councils have shown no interest in changing that equation even when opportunities to grow richer have occasionally presented themselves.

Culturally, Sope is often looked down on by its wealthier neighbors as backward or irrelevant, a judgment Sope's people have grown used to and largely stopped resenting. Being beneath notice, after all, has kept Sope free in a way that plenty of larger, prouder nations on Malagaerûn can no longer claim for themselves.
