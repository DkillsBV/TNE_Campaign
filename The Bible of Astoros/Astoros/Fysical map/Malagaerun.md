Malagaerûn is an island continent that has never quite decided what it wants to be — too crowded with old grudges to unify, too tangled by trade and marriage to fully separate. It reshapes itself through _inheritance_ more than conquest: titles split, dynasties fracture, and borders drift one broken engagement or contested will at a time.

#### Where Stone Turns to Sand

The continent's character changes as you travel it. In the north, where the land finally softens into dunes and open coastline, **Illipand** is the closest thing Malagaerûn has to a cradle of civilization — a realm of old magic and older families, its beaches and harbors having funded half the continent's wars without ever fighting one directly. Beside it, **East Masterg** and **West Masterg** were a single dukedom once, before a succession dispute a century ago split it down a mountain spine still marked by rival banners on either slope — the divide never healed, just calcified into two proud, prickly little states that despise each other more than they'd ever admit to outsiders. Inland from them, **Yonia** guards knowledge the way other nations guard gold, its scholars and hedge-mystics tolerated everywhere because no one wants to be on the wrong side of what they know.

Travel far enough south, and the coastline gives way entirely to peaks. The **Kingdom of Blothonevisson Dakury** holds no shoreline at all — a landlocked mountain realm whose wealth and defense are built on what can be mined and fortified rather than sailed or traded. Where Illipand promises open water, Blothonevisson Dakury promises the opposite: stone, altitude, and passes narrow enough that a hundred defenders who knew the ground could stop an army.

#### An Empire Testing Its Borders

No nation on Malagaerûn has grown faster or hungrier than the **Empire of Chatillinquis**. What began as a modest trading principality has, in the span of two generations, swallowed its neighbors through marriage, debt, and — where neither worked — the sword. Chatillinquis calls it "unification under the crown"; everyone else calls it what it is. Its influence now presses hard against **Kancandrith**, a land of minor lords, hill country, and superstitions older than any written law — a buffer state that has survived for centuries by being useful to everyone and loyal to no one, but that strategy grows harder to maintain the longer Chatillinquis keeps expanding at its border.

#### The Lands No One Fully Rules

South of Kancandrith, the **Kingdom of Marchrish** clings to its highlands with a stubbornness bordering on myth, having repelled three separate attempts at annexation through terrain alone rather than strength. **Vinzelkin**, sprawling and thinly governed, is less a unified nation than a patchwork of local rule stitched together under a crown that mostly exists on paper — its wild borderlands home to peoples the "civilized" kingdoms have spent generations trying to push out, driven onto land no one else wanted. **Hardsbury**, cold and industrious in equal measure, keeps largely to itself, its snowbound trade routes and stonework making it wealthy enough to ignore the politics happening south of it — for now.

#### Places Too Small to Conquer, Too Stubborn to Vanish

**Sope** is barely a nation at all by most definitions — a small, fiercely independent enclave that answers to no crown, protected less by walls than by the simple fact that it has nothing anyone wants badly enough to invade for. **Cred St Th-wes** is a port city-state that has made an art of neutrality, its harbors open to every flag on the continent and its loyalty available to whoever's paying — a useful ally, an unreliable one, and the closest thing Malagaerûn has to open ground. **Virefordy**, low and marshy, has turned its unwelcoming geography into its greatest defense, its causeways known only to locals and its people famously difficult to conquer simply because no one can find them.

#### A Quiet Held Together by Habit, Not Peace

Malagaerûn holds no grand alliance and no unifying threat sharp enough to force one — only old borders, older resentments, and one empire in the north growing faster than anyone's comfortable with. Blothonevisson Dakury has stayed carefully uninvolved in Chatillinquis's ambitions so far, its mountains giving it little reason to care what happens on the coasts — but every northern kingdom watching Chatillinquis grow has started to wonder how long that indifference will hold. Kancandrith sits closer to that growth than almost anywhere else, a hill country of minor lords and superstitious castles that has survived so far on neutrality and usefulness alone. Whether that's still enough is a question its people — lords and orphans alike — haven't had to answer yet.
