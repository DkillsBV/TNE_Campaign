Dravethis has never been a land of fixed lines. Its map is a patchwork of empires, republics, refuges, and accidents of history, and in the last few years alone it has begun reshaping itself faster than at any point most of its nations can remember.

### The Old Powers

Some nations here are simply _old_. **Walingand**, ruled by its ancient elven line, measures its history in centuries of patient survival rather than conquest — and only recently grew beyond its historic borders through a royal marriage that absorbed the war-weary halfling and gnome lands of Cerrehiven. **Untore**, arid and hospitable, has held its **First Water Pact** for generations, a founding principle of shared resources and open hospitality that now extends to the mysterious Dragonborn arriving from the distant Crimson Forests. **Firk**, steady and unremarkable by design, has outlasted crises through patience alone — a philosophy so deeply held that when its royal family split over ambition, it simply let the dissenters leave peacefully rather than fight, birthing the young, restless nation of **Virk** a decade ago.

### The Rising Threat

Nothing on the continent has moved faster than the **Empire of Am Aupeth**. Once a quiet matriarchal kingdom ruled by the Motherhouse, it has spent the last several years annexing neighbor after neighbor under a doctrine of "reunification," dressed in propaganda about bloodline and destiny while enforcing a brutal racial hierarchy beneath it. It has already swallowed border after border, torn a western province from **Unton** through engineered debt and manufactured crisis, and shows no sign of stopping — leaving nearly every nation south of Untore recalculating what safety actually requires.

### The Answer to That Threat

That recalculation is well underway. **Cangansov**, scarred by centuries of being fought over and having already lost Cerrehiven to Walingand, has thrown itself into permanent militarization, convinced that being ready before a threat arrives beats bleeding for territory after. **Unton**, dwarven and blindsided by its own losses, has abandoned its old complacency entirely, racing to fortify a border it never thought it would need. Watching from behind ice-bound walls, the goliath **Kingdom of Taginumn** — the continent's second strongest military, built on a warrior culture that never really stopped preparing — has begun quietly offering alliances to anyone willing to stand against Am Aupeth, understanding better than anyone that the Empire's appetite won't end at one border.

### Wildcards and Institutions

Not every nation fits neatly into that gathering conflict. **Manorn Nore** answers to no dynasty at all, its orcish throne changing hands whenever a challenger proves strong enough to take it in the **Reckoning**, backed by a real standing military that still runs on raw strength over bloodline. **Lansoomeshiz** is a nation of refugees who never meant to become one, surviving through invisibility rather than walls or armies. **Tuis**, born from a violent landing two hundred and fifty years ago, still hasn't decided whether it's a homeland or an occupation. **Grael**, wealthy from rare ore, governs through its unusual **Twin Seats** senate — every race represented equally regardless of numbers — and famously gave away land it couldn't use to secure Taginumn's lasting goodwill. **Ins**, gnomish and shaped by a famine its founders never forgot, has built an entire foreign policy around needing everyone and threatening no one. **Baval Pen** buys the outcomes it can't be bothered to fight for, its small, ruinously expensive Coinguard backed by the deepest treasury on the continent. And **Everg**, small and unguarded, is protected by something no army could replicate: it is the living holy seat of Dorvek, Seat of the Forge, and even an empire drunk on conquest has so far balked at being remembered as the power that put out his fire.

### A Continent Holding Its Breath

Taken together, Dravethis is a continent of old patience running up against new ambition — nations that spent centuries surviving through hospitality, neutrality, or sheer stubbornness now forced to decide whether that's still enough. Am Aupeth's Motherhouse shows no sign of slowing, its next target uncertain even to its own war councils. Whether Cangansov, Unton, and Taginumn's gathering resistance is enough to hold the line — or whether Dravethis is simply watching the opening chapter of the Empire's next century — is a question no nation on the map has an answer to yet.
