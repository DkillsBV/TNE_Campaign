_Crimson lands beneath the shadow of Untore_

One of the oldest nations on the continent, Walingand traces its founding to ancient elven waystone circles raised over eleven centuries ago, when the land was known as _Ael'wyn Sorreth_, "the Waiting Wood." While younger kingdoms rose through conquest, Walingand endured through patience — a small, insular realm respected by its neighbors for remembering what no other dynasty could.

That changed two generations ago, when the neighboring domain of **Cerrehiven** — home to halflings, gnome tinker-clans, and human foresters, worn down by border wars with Manorn Nore and Cangansov — offered its lord's daughter in marriage to Walingand's elder-queen **Sillevane Duskthorn** in exchange for protection. The union was sealed without a single battle, and Walingand grew larger than at any point in its recorded history.

Today the kingdom blends its ancient elven nobility with halfling burgomasters, gnome guildmasters, human foresters, and wandering orcs and tieflings drawn by its old neutrality — a nation now learning how to be large after centuries of simply enduring.

### The Verdant Wardens

Walingand keeps no standing army in the conventional sense — for most of its history it never needed one. Instead it relies on the **Verdant Wardens**, elven rangers and archers bound to the old forest circles, who patrol rather than garrison and strike from cover rather than hold a line. Doctrine favors ambush, attrition, and the terrain itself over open battle; Walingand has never won a war by outnumbering an enemy, only by outlasting one.

Since the union with **Cerrehiven**, the Wardens have absorbed halfling skirmishers and gnome-built trap-engineers, while a company of orcish mercenaries — the **Ashbrand Company** — serves as the kingdom's rare heavy infantry, hired generations ago and never quite let go. The army remains small by the standards of its rivals, but Walingand has always measured strength in centuries survived, not soldiers fielded.
