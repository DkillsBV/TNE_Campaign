_The grey uplands between Lansoomeshiz, Wesch, and Tuis_

### An Old, Steady Hand

Firk is not a nation known for drama, and it prefers it that way. Settled for centuries by a patchwork of highland clans that formalized into a single kingdom well before most of its neighbors had settled their own borders, Firk has built its reputation on being reliable rather than remarkable — steady trade, unspectacular but competent governance, and a foreign policy best summarized as _stay out of other people's wars long enough that they eventually need you for something_. It has watched Cangansov bleed, Manorn Nore squabble, and Am Aupeth rise with the detached patience of a nation that has learned, correctly, that outlasting a crisis is usually cheaper than winning one.

Firk's monarchy is old but deliberately unglamorous — a line of pragmatic rulers more interested in granaries and garrisons than conquest, governing a population of mostly **humans and gnomes**, with respected minority communities of **dwarves** in its eastern quarries and **halflings** in its river-valley farmsteads. It is, by most measures, the most unremarkable major power on the continent. That's precisely why it's still here.

### The Schism

Firk's one great disruption in living memory wasn't a war — it was a divorce. A generation ago, a succession dispute split the royal court down the middle: one faction wanted to continue Firk's cautious, inward-looking tradition; the other, led by a restless younger branch of the royal family, wanted Firk to finally act like the regional power its wealth suggested it could be — investing openly in its border territory near Wesch, fortifying it, and giving it a voice rather than treating it as a quiet backwater.

Rather than fight a civil war over it, Firk's crown did something almost unheard of for the region: it let the dissenting faction leave peacefully, granting the border territory formal independence rather than risk tearing the whole kingdom apart over an argument about ambition. That territory became **Virk** — the youngest nation on the continent, born not from conquest, refuge, or marriage, but from a family argument that both sides decided wasn't worth a war.
