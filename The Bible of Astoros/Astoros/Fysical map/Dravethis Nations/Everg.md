_The eastern coast beyond Ins and Grael_

### Fire and Faith

Everg is small, and has never once needed to be anything more. Where its neighbors measure themselves in territory, armies, or gold, Everg measures itself in two things it has never had to share the continent's respect for: the quality of what its forges produce, and the weight of what its temples hold. It is, by near-universal agreement, home to the finest blacksmiths in Dravethis — and the holiest seat on the continent for **Dorvek, Seat of the Forge**, god of smithing, craft, and oaths.

Everg's founding myth holds that Dorvek himself once walked among mortal smiths in the earliest age, dissatisfied — as he always is — with the first attempt at everything, and personally reforged the very ground the nation now stands on until it was worthy of holding his fire. Whether or not that's literal, Everg's smiths certainly work as though it is: nothing leaves an Everg forge on the first attempt, apprenticeships are built on Dorvek's own doctrine that _nothing good is made on the first try_, and every oath sworn in the nation — royal, marital, or contractual — is spoken over an anvil rather than a book, because in Everg an oath, like a blade, is something you build, not something you say.

### The Emberhearth and the Forgewarden

At the heart of Everg sits the **Emberhearth**, a temple-forge complex considered Dorvek's holiest site on the continent, tended by an order of priest-smiths known as the **Answerers** — Dorvek's clergy, who see every commission, sacred or mundane, as a kind of prayer completed with a hammer. Everg's head of state, the **Forgewarden**, is drawn from the senior ranks of the Answerers, chosen not through birthright but through a demonstration of craft the priesthood alone judges — part monarch, part high priest, part master smith, answering to Dorvek's doctrine as much as to any council. Fittingly for a nation devoted to a god who chairs the Celestial Senate's Committee on Revisions, Everg treats its own laws and rulings much like a blade: reviewed, reforged, and never assumed finished.

That religious weight makes Everg something unique on the continent: a nation too small and too poorly positioned to defend itself by force, and too spiritually significant for anyone to seriously consider attacking. Even Am Aupeth's doctrine of "reunification" has, so far, gone conspicuously quiet on the subject of Everg — invading the seat of the god half the continent's smiths, oath-takers, and armies quietly answer to is a cost even an empire drunk on conquest has been reluctant to pay.

### Its People

Everg is predominantly **dwarven**, its smithing tradition and priesthood both deeply rooted in dwarven craft-culture and a patient, generations-long relationship with fire and stone that maps neatly onto Dorvek's own doctrine of things "built," not rushed. A significant minority of **humans** serve as pilgrims-turned-settlers, merchants, and temple staff, drawn generations ago by faith and never quite leaving. **Gnomes** contribute heavily to the more intricate, ornamental, and mechanically ambitious commissions Everg is famous for — the pieces meant to be admired as much as used, and quietly considered by the Answerers to be Dorvek's own sense of craft taken to its playful extreme. A small, devout community of **tieflings** holds an unusually respected place within the priesthood itself, their presence woven into certain older forge-rites in ways the Answerers consider meaningful rather than troubling — a rare inversion of how they're treated almost everywhere else on the continent. A scattering of **halflings, elves, and orcs** round out the population, mostly pilgrims, traders, or apprentices who came for a blade or a blessing and simply stayed.

### Peace Bought by Reputation, Not Strength

Everg keeps only a small temple guard, more ceremonial than martial, and has never fielded anything resembling a real army — it has simply never needed one. Its true defense is reputation: every noble house on the continent wants an Everg blade, every oath-bound ruler wants that oath sealed at an Everg anvil, and no one, not even an empire built on conquest, wants to be remembered as the power that put out Dorvek's own fire. Everg has survived centuries of wars fought all around it not by winning any of them, but by making itself something every side, however bitterly opposed, still needs — and something the Seat of the Forge himself is said to be watching rather closely.
