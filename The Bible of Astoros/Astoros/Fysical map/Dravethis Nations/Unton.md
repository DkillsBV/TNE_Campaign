_The southern marches bordering the Empire of Am Aupeth_

### A Kingdom That Never Saw It Coming

Unton was, until recently, an unremarkable regional power — old enough to be settled, stable enough to be unbothered, and comfortable enough in its borders that it never seriously planned for losing them. It shares a long southern frontier with what used to be a modest, inward-looking matriarchal kingdom: the Empire of Am Aupeth. For generations that border was simply background noise, a line on old treaties nobody expected to matter.

That changed with terrifying speed. As Am Aupeth's doctrine of "reunification" swept through Cangansov's southern reaches and the smaller territories beyond, Unton assumed — as everyone did — that its own long-standing treaties and marriage-ties to the old Am Aupeth crown would keep it safe. It was wrong. Barely a year ago, a Handmaiden-Governor's "administrative dispute" over a western border province escalated into open annexation almost overnight — engineered debt, a manufactured succession crisis in the province's ruling house, and then Am Aupeth soldiers simply walking in to "restore order." Unton's western territories, along with a sizeable chunk of its population, its farmland, and no small amount of its pride, now answer to the Motherhouse.

### Too Slow, Now Too Late to Wait

The shock of that loss has reshaped Unton entirely. Where it once maintained a modest, ceremonial-heavy garrison force, the crown has thrown itself into open, urgent militarization — new levies raised in every hold, veteran officers pulled out of retirement, foundries running day and night to arm soldiers who, a year ago, nobody thought Unton would need. Diplomats have been dispatched to Cangansov, Wesch, and even distant Taginumn, testing whether old rivalries can be set aside against a common threat that has already proven it doesn't stop once it starts. Unton's leadership no longer debates _whether_ Am Aupeth will come for the rest of the border — only when, and whether Unton will be ready.

There's a bitter mood running through the kingdom now, equal parts fear and fury: Unton was not conquered by strength it couldn't have matched, but by an enemy it simply refused to take seriously until it was already too late for the province it lost. That mistake is not one its generals intend to repeat twice.

### Its People

Unton is, and has long been, a **dwarf**-majority kingdom — its holds carved into the southern highlands running down toward the Am Aupeth border, its wealth and identity built on generations of quarrying, forge-work, and fortress-building. That heritage has suddenly become the kingdom's most valuable asset: Unton's dwarven engineers and stoneworkers, once mostly occupied with mines and monuments, are now working around the clock raising fortifications along a border everyone assumed would never need them.

**Humans** make up a sizeable minority, filling much of the farmland and river valleys and the bulk of the newly raised levies. **Halflings** work the same farming villages, many now displaced north as refugees from the fallen western province. **Gnomes** serve prominently in Unton's engineering corps, increasingly redirected from civil works toward siege-craft, and a scattering of **orcs**, drawn by steady military wages, now fill out the expanded frontier regiments alongside Unton's own hastily trained recruits.

Unton is not yet at war. But it is, for the first time in its history, a nation that has stopped assuming peace is the default — and every fortress going up along its shrinking southern border, hewn from dwarven stone by dwarven hands, says so.
