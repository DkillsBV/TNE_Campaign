_The arid highlands north of Lansoomeshiz_

### The First Water Pact

Untore is a land that shouldn't, by rights, have produced one of the continent's most peaceful nations — arid desert, broken hill country, and valleys hidden between rock rather than carved by any river worth naming. For centuries its scattered human tribes, along with the dwarf and goliath clans who shared the same unforgiving hills, survived on nothing but a handful of wells and springs, and fought over them the way anyone would when the alternative is dying of thirst.

That changed after a drought remembered simply as **the Long Dry**, severe enough to break tribes that had never bent to anything before. Rather than let the last surviving wells become the spoils of whichever tribe held them, Untore's elders did something no one on the continent had managed before or since: they agreed, in what's now called the **First Water Pact**, that water belonged to everyone and to no one — no ruler, no tribe, no claim of first-arrival. It's a simple agreement that has held, unbroken, for generations, and it shaped everything Untore became afterward.

### A Cup of Water for Anyone

Untore's culture still runs on that founding pact, extended far past the wells it was written for. Hospitality here isn't courtesy — it's closer to law. Travelers are offered water and shelter regardless of where they've come from or who they answer to, a custom summed up in the old Untorean proverb: _in the desert, even an enemy deserves a cup of water._ In a region increasingly defined by Am Aupeth's conquests and Cangansov's fortifications, Untore has stayed almost stubbornly outside that pattern — a nation that has simply never found a reason to stop sharing what it once nearly died fighting over.

### The Newcomers from the Crimson Forests

In recent years, Untore's hospitality has been tested by something genuinely new: the arrival of **Dragonborn**, migrating in increasing numbers from the distant Crimson Forests far to the north. Why they've come isn't entirely settled. Some describe themselves plainly as refugees; others frame the journey as simple opportunity, a new life along Untore's trade routes; a few, more unsettlingly, simply say that something in the Crimson Forests changed, and that staying stopped being an option. None of them agree on what that something was.

True to the Pact, Untore has welcomed them without much hesitation, and Dragonborn communities have begun taking root around its larger towns and caravan routes, bringing crafts, customs, and stories that Untore's older population finds equal parts fascinating and faintly unsettling. Nobody in Untore has pressed the newcomers too hard for answers — hospitality doesn't come with an interrogation attached — but the quiet question of what actually happened in the Crimson Forests hasn't gone away. It's simply been set aside, for now, in favor of pouring another cup of water.

### The Hillwardens

Untore's defense reflects the same philosophy as everything else about it: modest, practical, and built around the land rather than against it. The **Hillwardens** are a small standing force, tasked less with conquest or grand battle than with protecting settlements and, above all, the water sources the entire nation still considers sacred. They rely on knowledge of the desert's hidden valleys, flash floods, and shifting terrain rather than numbers or heavy arms — a force built to make invading Untore more trouble than it's worth, rather than to win a war outright.

Untore has never needed a large army to stay safe. It has simply made itself the one nation on the continent that almost no one has a reason to hate.
