_The frozen bastion beyond Firk and Unton_

### Winter's Own Kingdom

Taginumn is the coldest nation on the continent, sitting at a near-constant chill of around 5°C even at the height of its short summers — a climate few races can call comfortable, but that Taginumn's people have never treated as a hardship. The kingdom's fortress-capital sits behind walls of quarried ice-stone that have never fallen to a besieging army — not because no one has tried, but because no army has ever managed to keep a siege alive through a Taginumn winter. The cold itself is Taginumn's oldest soldier, and its people have simply learned to be its allies rather than its victims.

This is a kingdom built on old oaths rather than shifting politics. Its great houses swear fealty not merely to the crown but to the land itself, in ceremonies barely changed in centuries, before standing stones older than most of Dravethis's written history. Taginumn does not chase glory or expansion — it has never needed to. It endures, the way it always has, and lets softer nations wear themselves out chasing things Taginumn was never interested in.

### Its People

Taginumn is, and has always been, an overwhelmingly **goliath** kingdom — one of the only climates on the continent where their kind doesn't merely survive but genuinely thrives, and the cold that keeps other races huddled indoors is, to a goliath, simply home. Goliath physiology shaped the nation as much as the nation shaped its people: towering builds suited to the cold, a cultural obsession with endurance and personal trial, and settlements built wide and sturdy rather than tall, meant to shrug off ice and wind rather than hide from them.

Other races live here, but sparsely, and always on goliath terms. **Dwarves** hold a respected minority in the northern mountains, mining and smithing alongside kin they consider distant cousins in toughness. A small, hardened population of **humans** works the coastal ice-fishing fleets and short growing seasons, tough by necessity rather than by nature. A scattering of **orcs** serve with distinction in the frontier watches, respected for enduring conditions that break lesser soldiers, and an old, insular community of **elves** — remnants of a much older northern migration — keeps to the kingdom's ancient standing-stone groves. **Halflings, gnomes, and tieflings** are rare enough here to be a genuine curiosity; the cold simply isn't kind to them, and those who do stay are, without exception, tougher than they look.

### The Second Sword of the Continent

By quiet, universal agreement, Taginumn fields the second-strongest military power in Dravethis — a standing force built not from recent panic, like Unton's hurried levies, but from a warrior tradition that never really stopped preparing. Every noble house maintains its own hardened retinue year-round, built around goliath shock-infantry that need no special training to withstand conditions that would fell any other army outright. Taginumn doesn't raise armies for war. It simply has one, always, the way it always has firewood stacked before the first snow.

Word of Am Aupeth's southern conquests has been met not with panic, like Unton, but with grim, unsurprised readiness — the reaction of a people who always assumed the south would eventually produce something worth preparing for. Taginumn's war-council has spent the last year quietly stockpiling, drilling, and fortifying, while sending cold, blunt offers of alliance to Unton and Cangansov alike. Taginumn does not particularly like either nation. It simply understands that the Empire's appetite will not stop at one border, and it would rather fight beside allies on someone else's ground than alone on its own.

### The Long Watch

Taginumn's culture runs on duty and endurance rather than ambition — a people who genuinely believe that surviving with honor matters more than winning with none. Its warriors take the **Long Oath**, a lifetime vow to the crown and the cold alike, and its commanders are chosen for discipline and endurance over charisma or birthright. To the rest of Dravethis, Taginumn can seem distant, stern, and a little frightening — a kingdom of giants who watch the world's wars from behind ice-bound walls, waiting, the way they always have, to see who's still standing when the weather turns.
