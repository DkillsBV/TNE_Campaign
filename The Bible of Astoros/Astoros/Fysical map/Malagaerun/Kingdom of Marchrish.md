Marchrish has never won a war through numbers, wealth, or magic — it has won every war it's fought through sheer refusal to lose on its own terrain. The kingdom occupies some of the most punishing highlands on Malagaerûn, a maze of ridgelines, narrow passes, and weather that turns without warning, and every invading force that has ever marched into Marchrish has eventually discovered that the mountains were a better defender than any army the kingdom could field. Three separate attempts at annexation — two by ambitious neighbors, one distant memory of an older empire's expansion — have all ended the same way: not in decisive defeat, but in slow, grinding attrition until the invaders simply couldn't afford to continue.

This has given Marchrish's people a particular kind of pride, bordering on myth. Stories of the mountains "choosing sides" are told with total sincerity in Marchrish's villages, and its warriors are raised on tales of ancestors who needed nothing but knowledge of the terrain to turn back armies ten times their size. Whether or not there's any truth to the more supernatural versions of these stories, the practical result is the same: Marchrish's highland militias know every pass, every ambush point, and every place where a small, well-placed force can hold off a much larger one, and they train for little else.

Marchrish's government reflects its terrain — decentralized, clan-based, and deeply suspicious of any authority that tries to govern from a single distant seat. Local clan-lords hold real power in their own valleys, and the kingdom's nominal crown exists mostly to coordinate defense in times of genuine crisis rather than to rule day-to-day life. This makes Marchrish slow to act as a unified nation and nearly impossible to conquer as one, since defeating "Marchrish" would require defeating dozens of stubborn, independently-minded highland clans one at a time.

Marchrish watches Kancandrith's slow absorption into Chatillinquis's orbit with open unease, since a Chatillinquis foothold on Kancandrith's southern border would put the empire uncomfortably close to Marchrish's own lowland approaches. So far, the kingdom's response has been to do what it always does — fortify, wait, and trust the mountains to handle whatever comes next the way they always have.
